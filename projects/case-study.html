Case Study

Reporting Quality Improvement

An anonymized case study showing how workflow analysis, data validation and standardization reduced recurring reporting errors.

Context

Recurring errors were affecting the reliability of operational reporting. Different teams interpreted data fields differently, source information was not always validated and manual steps created avoidable inconsistencies.

My responsibility

Partner with stakeholders to understand the reporting flow, identify recurring failure points and introduce practical controls without slowing down weekly delivery.

Analysis

  • Mapped the end-to-end reporting workflow and source dependencies.
  • Reviewed common exception categories and where they entered the process.
  • Compared source values with reported outputs through reconciliation checks.
  • Clarified KPI definitions and ownership with contributing teams.

Intervention

  • Standardized data definitions and reporting templates.
  • Added validation controls before consolidation.
  • Introduced recurring reconciliation checks across reporting cycles.
  • Documented correction steps and escalation ownership.

Result

Reporting errors were reduced by approximately 25%, and stakeholders had a clearer, more consistent basis for weekly performance discussions.

What I learned

Data-quality improvements are most sustainable when process ownership, definitions and controls are addressed together rather than treating each error as an isolated incident.

Portfolio note: The case study is intentionally high level and anonymized. It excludes confidential data, screenshots and proprietary process details.
